# Animated world production direction

User clarified the earlier twenty-times suggestion: not necessarily hundreds of clips; prioritize lots of variety and animation smoothness. This clarification supersedes numeric minimums and the provisional 400-clip interpretation.

## Scale and counting

No fixed per-item animation quota. Build complete, convincing core actions first, then meaningful alternate idles, reactions, transitions and environmental behaviors. Frames are not separate animations. The JSON catalogue is planning vocabulary, not generated inventory or a mandatory checklist. Its 140 starter action names do not represent completed assets. Do not pad counts with duplicates or retiming.

Every item needs its own coverage plan. The family lists are starting vocabulary, not a universal checklist to force onto every object. A rock can respond through moss, droplets, insects, surface light, weather and interaction; it need not wobble. A tree needs different rhythms from a soldier. Replace unsuitable generic actions with meaningful item-specific ones while preserving variety and smoothness. Do not add game mechanics merely because a visual action exists.

## Coherence before volume

Prove one Greek fighter and one small environment patch against the selected pixel references. First verify a complete cycle, consistent equipment, transparent extraction, scale and pivot. Then broaden directions and variants where the result visibly benefits. Choose frame counts and timing for the action; extra frames alone do not guarantee smoothness. Check spacing, easing, weight transfer, anticipation, contact and recovery. Do not hide discontinuities with automatic interpolation or repeated endpoint frames.

Each clip has ordered frames and individual timings, a stable pivot, facing, loop/recovery behavior and named visual event markers. Generated sprite grids are candidates until extracted and played. True alpha is preferred; otherwise use a flat chroma color absent from the palette. Do not key a light background against ivory cloth or skin highlights. Keep shadows separate. Review against light, dark and actual terrain.

## A living world that remains readable

Units have breathing, anticipation, recovery and personality. Architecture has doors, banners, torches, occupants and visible reactions. Vegetation responds to wind, rain and contact. Water has currents, foam, ripples and splashes. Small props and effects follow their own material behavior.

Use long quiet holds, varied phase offsets and coordinated wind/weather events. Do not start every loop simultaneously. Avoid constant full-screen motion: battle cues need visual priority. Freeze offscreen animation work, use shared atlases where coherent, and schedule ambient motion less often at distance. Provide reduced ambient motion without hiding essential combat feedback.

The simulation remains authoritative. Presentation event markers synchronize visuals; they do not decide damage or resource spending. Cosmetic clip selection uses a separate deterministic seed and never consumes gameplay randomness.

## Production records

See ANIMATION_CATALOGUE.json for starter families and required clip metadata. Track planned, generated, rejected, accepted and integrated counts separately. Only accepted clips count toward delivered coverage; quality and useful variety govern acceptance, not clip totals. Preserve sources and provenance. No paid generation; Magnific browser generation only with visibly confirmed Generate Unlimited. Bulk volume does not authorize paid tools or unchecked submissions.

Current checkpoint is direction and catalogue only. No sprite animations have been generated or integrated by this change.
